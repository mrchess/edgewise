macOS touch driver

Taps land where you touch.

The Corsair Xeneon Edge is a 14.5-inch, 2560×720 touchscreen. On macOS it behaves like a trackpad, because macOS has no idea what it is. Edgewise fixes that.

The problem

macOS treats it as a mouse, not a screen

When macOS meets a HID digitizer it doesn't recognise, it falls back to reading it as a relative pointing device. Your finger nudges the cursor from wherever it already sat, instead of moving it to where you touched. Corsair's iCUE solves this on Windows and has never existed for the Mac.

The cursor creeps from where it was. You aim, then re-aim.

After

The click lands under your finger. Every time.

What it does

The gestures this panel can do

Tap

Clicks exactly where you touched.

Double-tap

A real double-click — opens folders, selects words.

Press and hold

Right-click. Hold time is adjustable.

Drag

Press, move, release — windows and selections.

Your cursor ends up where it started

A tap moves the cursor to your finger, clicks, and returns it — about 20ms, one frame — so you can hit a control on the panel without losing your place on the main display. An earlier version posted events straight to the app underneath to skip the trip entirely. It needed a private API, Chromium apps discarded the events anyway, and the end state was identical. It was removed.

Beyond touch

Turn the panel into an app strip

The panel, running the strip — a real capture, not a mockup.

Switch it on and the panel becomes a row of app buttons: tap one and that app comes forward. Add, remove and reorder them in settings; give the strip the whole panel or a left/right half, third, quarter or eighth, with the rest left as ordinary desktop you can still touch; and lay the buttons out in a fixed number of rows or let them wrap on their own.

Optionally, tapping an icon moves the cursor onto the app it brings forward, so you land on it pointer and all. It activates the app and stops there — it does not chase the caret into a text box. That was tried and dropped on purpose: the apps people reach for either focus their own input on activation, or hide it behind a contenteditable no accessibility walk can find.

The hardware

Multi-finger gestures cannot work on macOS

Its descriptor declares a full ten-finger digitizer. In practice it transmits only on a second, mouse-emulation interface describing a single pointer — across thousands of captured reports, the digitizer sent nothing at all. That is why multi-finger gestures do not work here, and probably why the panel's multi-touch is widely assumed to be a hardware limitation. 

// 1,100+ reports captured during taps and two-finger swipes
hid digitizer=false  count=1
hid digitizer=false  count=1101
// the digitizer interface never spoke

On Windows the host writes SET_REPORT Feature 0x21 = [21 02 00] and multitouch starts streaming about 3ms later. Myseri's project captured that exchange and built a DriverKit driver that replays it byte-for-byte — every response byte identical to Windows'. No effect. They also tried both SET_REPORT framings at both the HID and raw USB layers, and UPDD, the commercial touch driver, claiming the whole device exclusively. Still single-touch.

Three independent stacks fail identically. The surviving explanation is that the firmware fingerprints the host OS during bus enumeration, before any driver loads, and honours the mode switch only when it believes it is talking to Windows.

What this means in practice

Tap, drag, double-tap and press-and-hold work today, on the correct display. The gesture recogniser already handles multiple contacts and is unit-tested for them, and Edgewise still attempts the mode switch at startup — so if a firmware or OS change ever allows it, two-finger scroll and pinch begin working without a rewrite.

Testing

Record once, replay forever

Touch hardware can't be present on a CI runner, so the driver records one. The core is pure — parsing, mapping, display resolution and gesture recognition do no I/O and take “now” from the caller — and real gestures are captured as HID fixtures and replayed through the production pipeline with a virtual clock. 

# capture a real gesture from the panel
edgewise-diag record fixtures/slow-drag.json 8

# replay it through the real parser and recogniser
edgewise-diag replay fixtures/slow-drag.json
  drag began  (1204, 1622)
  drag moved  (1361, 1622)
  drag ended  (2287, 1623)

Every timing rule — long-press, momentum decay, the stuck-contact timeout — is tested without a single sleep.
